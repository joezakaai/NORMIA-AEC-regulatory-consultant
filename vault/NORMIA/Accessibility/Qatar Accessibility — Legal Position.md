# Qatar Accessibility — Legal Position

> [!info] [[NORMIA]] · [[Qatar]] · [[Accessibility]] · [[Authority Directory]]

> [!danger] There is a mandatory duty but no technical standard defining compliance
> Qatar has **no confirmed national built-environment accessibility code**. Extensive searching
> found no titled Qatari technical accessibility standard with an issuing body and edition.
> What exists is a **statute with no technical content** plus **voluntary/sectoral guidance**.

## Law No. (22) of 2025 on Persons with Disabilities — the major development

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Enacted | **5 October 2025** |
| Effective | **19 November 2025** |
| Repeals | **Law No. 2 of 2004** on Persons with Disabilities |
| Status | **Mandatory statute** |

Built-environment language, quoted from the source:

> "the design of workplaces and facilities must take accessibility into account, and necessary
> modifications must be made **within one year from the date the law takes effect**."

and a requirement for:

> "barrier-free workplaces, customer areas, communication systems, and online services."

**Implied compliance deadline: around 19 November 2026.**

On the technical criteria:

> "The Council of Ministers is expected to issue executive regulations under the Law in the
> coming months."

> [!warning] The executive regulations are the critical open item
> They are the most likely vehicle for Qatar's first binding accessibility criteria. **I could not
> confirm whether they have been issued.** Until they are, a mandatory accessibility duty exists
> with no published technical standard defining compliance.

⚠️ **These details rest on a single third-party law-firm source. The primary Arabic text was not
fetched.** Verify the article numbering and the exact deadline against the Official Gazette before
relying on them.

## What exists instead of a code

| Body / instrument | Nature |
| --- | --- |
| **Mada Center** | *"the first organization in the country to officially provide Accessible Built Environment consultation services"*; *"advises public and private entities on inclusive design based on global standards like the ADA"*. **Advisory, not regulatory.** |
| **Accessible Qatar** (Sasol + Ministry of Social Development and Family) | **Voluntary** venue-auditing app/initiative. Publishes *Accessible Events Guidelines* (Sept 2018) and *Accessible Stand Guidelines* (Feb 2018), both *"provided as a courtesy to the community"* |
| **Qatar Tourism Authority Classification Manual v1.1 (2018)** | Hotel grading — **sectoral**, not a building code |
| **Qatar eAccessibility Policy (2011)** | **ICT/digital only** — not built environment |

> [!tip] The strongest evidence that no domestic code exists
> Mada — Qatar's designated accessibility body — explicitly benchmarks to the **ADA**. And Mada's
> own policy portal hosts the **Saudi** built-environment accessibility guidelines, with **no
> Qatari equivalent**.

## Design Consequence

For any project with an accessibility requirement in Qatar, NORMIA cannot cite a national code
because there isn't one. The practical position:

1. **Agree the standard with the authority or client in writing** at pre-application. Do not
   assume ADA, ISO 21542 or BS 8300 applies — none is adopted by any Qatari instrument found.
2. Where a master developer imposes a requirement, that is the binding one — e.g. Lusail requires
   *"barrier-free access in accordance with recognized best international practice"*, which is
   itself not a citable standard. See [[Lusail — Universal Access and Accessible Parking]].
3. **Watch the Law 22/2025 executive regulations** and the ~November 2026 modification deadline for
   existing workplaces and facilities.
