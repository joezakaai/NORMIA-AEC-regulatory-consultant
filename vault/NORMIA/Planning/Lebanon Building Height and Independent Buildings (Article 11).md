# Lebanon — Building Height, Floor Count and Independent Buildings
### المادة الحادية عشرة · decree pp. 48–50

Parent: [[Lebanon Building Law Implementing Decree 15874-2005]]

> [!important] Why independence matters
> Whether two masses on one plot are **one** building or **two independent** buildings decides how height is measured and how floors are counted. It is the single most consequential classification in this article.

## The independence test — ثانياً/١

| Classification | Test |
|---|---|
| **البناء المستقل** — independent building | **D ≥ 2 × R**, with an absolute minimum of **4.50 m** |
| **البناء غير المستقل** — non-independent | **D < 4.50 m** OR **D < 2 × R** |

where **D** = the distance between the two masses and **R** = the largest imposed setback.

For non-independent buildings the floor count is taken across the whole, and Figure 52 gives the rule:

| Condition | Floors counted |
|---|---|
| **d ≤ 5.75 m** | **n = 3** |
| **d > 5.75 m** | **n = 4** |

## Buried lower floors

Buried lower floors (**الطوابق السفلية المردومة**) **may be continuous** between otherwise independent blocks — continuity below ground does not defeat independence above it. Figure 55 shows the separation condition, printed as **d' ≥ 4.50 m**.

> [!warning] Source defect — recorded, not reinterpreted
> Figure 55 prints the inequality as **`4.50m ≤ D ≤ 2×R`**, which is not obviously consistent with the independence rule **D ≥ 2R, min 4.50 m** stated in the text of ثانياً/١. Reproduced verbatim from the drawing; **the governing text should be preferred and the point put to the administration.**
