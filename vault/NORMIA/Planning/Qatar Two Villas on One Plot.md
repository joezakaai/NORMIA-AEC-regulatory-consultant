# Qatar Two Villas on One Plot

> [!info] [[NORMIA]] · [[Qatar]] · [[Planning]] · [[QNMP Zoning Regulation Framework]]
> Ministry of Municipality & Environment · Ref. **281326/2014**, dated **01/10/2014**

## Requirement

Quoted verbatim from the official PDF:

> "Building coverage shall not exceed 60% of the plot area and a maximum floor area ratio (FAR)
> of 1.4"
>
> "No more than one electricity meter with 160 ampere electric capacity will be allowed"
>
> Palace zones: maximum coverage of "50% of the plot area"

## Value / Criterion

| Control | Value | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building coverage (max) | **60%** of plot area | 50% in palace zones |
| **FAR (max)** | **1.4** | ⚠️ **Conflicts with R1/R2 FAR 1.65** |
| Electricity meters | **1**, max **160 A** | A planning control enforced through the utility connection |

The document does **not** set setbacks, heights, minimum plot size or parking — those fall back to
the underlying zone regulation.

## Design Consequence

The one-meter/160 A limit is the sharpest control here and is easy to overlook: two villas on one
plot are treated as **one connection**. That constrains total electrical load across both
dwellings and has direct consequences for cooling plant selection. Raise it with the MEP engineer
at concept stage, not at Kahramaa submission.

## Conflicts / Interactions

> [!danger] Unreconciled FAR conflict
> This guidance gives **FAR 1.4** at 60% coverage (2014).
> [[Qatar R1 — Low Density Residential Zone]] and [[Qatar R2 — Low-Medium Density Residential Zone]]
> give **FAR 1.65** at the same 60% coverage (2020).
>
> The zone regulations are six years newer; this guidance is use-specific. **Which governs a
> two-villa application is not resolved anywhere in the published set.** Design to **1.4** — the
> more restrictive reading — until the Urban Planning Department rules, and record the assumption.
